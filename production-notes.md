The highly idiosyncratic numbering system in this work poses a special challenge to markup and CSS. Because some subitems are 0-indexed and others not, we can't use CSS counters. Ideally we would mark things up semantically in a series of nested `<ol>` and `<li>` elements; but without modern CSS like CSS grids, positioning the numbers becomes impossible. (epub cannot use `position: absolute;` and Kindle does not support `display: table;`). Ideally we might give each `<li>` element a `data-index` attribute containing the decimal string, and fill a `::before` element with that value. But, at the time of ebook production, Kindle doesn't support `attr()` CSS. So, we use a plain table, for the time being.

The notation for equations posed a considerable problem. Wittgenstein's notation is unlike modern notation, and thus it was difficult to attempt to give semantic MathML meaning to the underlying equations. Therefore they have been rendered in presentational MathML (vs. content MathML). Semantic grouping has been attempted with `<mrow>` elements but these should not be considered authoritative, or even correct. Regardless of the underlying semantics, the MathML should render just as in print. This page was helpful in unpacking the meaning of several of the equations: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/267638339_Wittgenstein's_Tractatus_logico-philosophicus_glossary_of_mathematical_and_logical_symbols

Punctuation has been left outside of quotation marks in many cases for clarity, even though the SE manual specifies that punctuation goes inside quotation marks. Because many equations and variables are within double quotes, it is more clear to leave grammatical punctuation, which may be confused with logical operators, outside of such quotes.
